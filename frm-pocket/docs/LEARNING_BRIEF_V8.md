# Adaptive FRM II preparation — expanded implementation brief

## Outcome

Help Charaf prepare for FRM Part II on 24 November 2026, with short mobile sessions and deeper work outside transport. Improve recall, conceptual understanding, problem solving and critical judgment. The app supports preparation; it cannot establish an exam pass probability or guarantee success.

## Curriculum and source discipline

- Use all 107 current readings across the six 2026 domains as the roadmap backbone.
- Review the five supplied 2024 core books for chapter content, equations, problems, assumptions and traps. Record which chapters correspond to current readings, which moved or are supplementary, and which current readings are absent.
- Treat 2026 Current Issues and newer assigned readings separately. Use the current official objectives and primary sources, and disclose unavailable source sections.
- Keep uploaded PDFs private and out of both deployments. Publish only original authored study material and chapter/page references.
- Do not reproduce official questions or claim difficulty has been calibrated against the real exam. Create new scenarios and checks rather than numerical reskins of existing items.

## Learning packs

Every current reading needs a concise conceptual guide, retrieval prompts, diagnosed traps, a worked application with a plausibility check, and related original questions. Formula records explain variables, units, assumptions and common misuses. A governance reading need not acquire an artificial equation.

Questions separate understanding, application and critical reasoning. Hard questions should require choosing assumptions, identifying information that is insufficient, checking boundary cases, tracing cash flows and distinguishing financial mechanisms from misleading shortcuts. Distractors represent plausible errors and receive individual explanations.

## Adaptive roadmap

- Use current, observed first-attempt evidence, independently delayed recall, unresolved errors, confidence on incorrect answers, mock-reported weak readings and optional self-assessment.
- Keep self-declared reading, free-text explanations and card ratings separate from objective performance. Do not automatically grade a written explanation as true understanding.
- Expose uncertainty: unknown chapters stay unknown; small samples stay limited. Missing dimensions and stale evidence remain visible.
- Explain why a chapter has priority. Suggest prerequisites without locking chapters.
- Recompute the order and near-term projection when evidence changes. Respect retry deadlines, the exam date, daily budgets, four-hour mocks and final light days.
- Show every current reading even when the remaining time cannot allocate a separate consolidation slot to all of them. Do not equate a 25-minute step with learning an entire new chapter.
- Preserve the existing global readiness /100 method and history. Add clearly distinguished chapter evidence indices and real chapter history; never reconstruct a fictional past.

## Mobile, math and continuity

Retain the established design and touch controls. Render mathematical expressions locally with LaTeX, including worked reasoning and formula conditions. Cache the expanded content, renderer and fonts for offline use after an online update. Preserve old question IDs, answers, local storage key, notes, active sessions and backups. Keep the 40 reserved checkpoint questions separated from practice and cards.

## Acceptance checks

Validate all reading mappings, unique IDs/stems, four choices and corresponding rationales, formula syntax and independent numerical computations. Test adaptive priorities, changing projections, sparse evidence, future/expired records, delayed recall, assessment protections, backup migration and offline cache updates. Record the limits of source review and physical-device testing honestly.
